01The Idea

Professional and personal, at once.

The tagline says one half, "bringing professionalism back to real estate." The logo says the other, "like family." The whole identity lives in holding them together. All professional reads cold and corporate; all personal reads soft and amateur. The tension between them is the brand.

Professional

The expertise half

  • Premier, upscale, waterfront market
  • Technology, privacy, speed and efficiency
  • Clean structure, confident type, blue accent
  • Language about skill and the process
Personal

The family half

  • "Treated as though I were family"
  • Two named people, local roots, real faces
  • Warm neutrals, natural light, human moments
  • Language about care and relationships

Every page should feel competent enough to trust with a $2M sale, and warm enough to call back.

02Positioning

Anne and Sean, front and centre.

Most real estate sites lead with a stock photo of a house. This one leads with two named people and a family promise, the one thing competitors cannot copy. Sean grew up in Martin County and knows the area the way a transplant never will; Anne is the steady, caring hand clients describe as treating them "like family." That pairing is the story every page should carry.

Lead with

  • Anne and Sean by name, with real faces
  • The "like family" promise, stated plainly
  • Sean's local, grew-up-here roots
  • Responsiveness and genuine care
  • Real recent sales on the Treasure Coast

Avoid

  • Stock real estate photography of any kind
  • Empty luxury filler like "dream home awaits"
  • Leading with price or "just listed" urgency
  • Anonymous "our team of experts"
  • Over-punctuation and hard-sell exclamation

Proof worth protecting: recent closings from $1.19M to $2.55M across Hobe Sound, Sewalls Point and Jensen Beach, full-time local experience, and a service style built on privacy, speed and personal attention.

03Colour

One coastal blue, a clean ground.

A confident coastal blue is the single accent, on a crisp ink-and-paper neutral system. Blue carries links, buttons and emphasis; everything else stays quiet so the blue and the photography lead. Warm sand is a soft touch of the "family" half; red is for alerts only.

05Layout

The page alternates, light then dark.

The structural rule is the band rhythm: paper and mist sections, with the occasional ink or blue band for weight. Alternation keeps a long page moving and gives the heavy moments, pricing, the family promise, the closing call to action, somewhere to land. Never stack two dark bands back to back.

Section marker

A large, low-opacity Fahkwang number sits behind each section header, giving the long page rhythm and a luxury-editorial feel.

Italic emphasis
Headline pattern

Every headline italicises one phrase in Fahkwang. One phrase, never two. It is what makes the headings feel written.

Full-bleed spread
Image moment

A property photo running edge to edge with a single pull quote. Used once or twice to let the page breathe.

The price ledger
Listing pattern

Name left, figure right, a hairline between rows. No boxes, no badges. Honest and plain.

06Photography

Bright, coastal, and real.

Photography should feel like the Treasure Coast on its best day: airy, light-filled and aspirational, but believable. Favour whites, soft blues and natural greens. When people are in frame, keep it warm and candid. Mismatched, over-filtered stock is the fastest way to undo the brand.

Do

  • Real listings and local waterfront, in natural light
  • Light-filled interiors, pools, docks, blue water
  • Anne and Sean shot the same way, eye level
  • Leave clean space for type to sit
  • Keep skin tones warm and true

Don't

  • Generic stock houses, even as placeholder
  • Heavy filters or cold blue grading
  • Busy backgrounds fighting the subject
  • Mismatched portrait styles on the team page
  • Tight crops with no breathing room
07Voice

Expert, warm, never pushy.

Write the way Anne or Sean would talk to someone across the kitchen table: confident about the market, genuinely interested in the person. Professional in substance, personal in tone. First person plural, no hard sell, no exclamation-point urgency.

Sounds like The Quinn Group

  • "We'll treat you like family, from the first call to the closing table."
  • "Sean grew up here. He knows these streets."
  • "We'll tell you honestly what a home is worth."
  • "Your move, handled with privacy and care."

Doesn't

  • "Your DREAM HOME is waiting, act now!!!"
  • "Lowest commissions in town."
  • Dense jargon and high-pressure lines
  • "Dear valued customer" and other filler

09Logo

The mark, and how to protect it.

The "Anne & Sean, like family" wordmark is the primary identity. Give it room, keep contrast high, and leave it alone. Dark mark on light grounds; reversed mark on blue or over photography.

Primary · dark mark on light grounds

Reversed · white mark on blue or photography

Clear space & size. Keep clear space on all sides equal to the cap-height of the wordmark; no text, rules or photo edges enter it. Minimum 140px wide on screen. Pair with the Equal Housing Opportunity mark where required.

× Don't stretch or condense
× Don't recolour the mark
× Don't put dark on dark
× Don't rotate or angle it
× No shadows or glows
10Social

Where most clients meet you.

More people will find Anne and Sean on Instagram than on the website, so the feed carries the same two halves: real local work and homes, held together by a disciplined, upscale frame.

Profile & grid

  • Reversed logo on blue, centred, legible at 32px
  • Alternate listings with team and coastal shots
  • Never three of the same kind in a row
  • Keep blue to one accent element per frame

Captions & handle

  • Brand voice, first person plural, warm
  • Name the neighbourhood; tag the location
  • Get client permission before posting a home
  • Use one handle consistently across platforms
11Components

Buttons, forms, and the small stuff.

Interface pieces stay simple and consistent. Primary actions are coastal blue; secondary actions are a blue outline. One corner radius, generous padding, clear focus.

Buttons

Start your searchGet in touch
Home valuation

Blue for the main action, outline for secondary, ink for a quiet alternate. Never more than one primary in a view.

Forms

Uppercase labels in grey, one hairline border, blue focus. Keep contact forms short.

Navigation & motion

  • Nav in Open Sans, calm and uncrowded; logo left, a single primary CTA right.
  • Links shift to Deep Blue on hover. No colour changes that fight the accent.
  • Motion is subtle: gentle fades and short eases. Nothing bouncy.
12Naming

Name, numbers, and metadata.

One spelling of everything, everywhere, from the footer to the Google listing. Consistency here is what makes a two-person team look like an established brand.
